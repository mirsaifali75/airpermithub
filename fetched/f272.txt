General Permit Program
October 24, 2024 
| 
Agency 
The Division of Air Pollution Control (DAPC) has developed model general permits-to-install (GP) and model general permits-to-install and operate (PTIO) for select sources in Ohio. The regulations for general permits can be found in Ohio Administrative Code (OAC) rule 3745-31-29 . 
Currently, there are more than 60 air pollution source types that are eligible for coverage under a general permit-to-install and/or a general permit-to-install and operate (PTIO). The general permits contain all the same requirements that would normally be included in traditional permits, but they can be obtained much more quickly.
For sources that qualify for a general permit, applicants must agree to meet pre-defined permit terms, including installation and/or operating requirements, related to items such as emissions limits, record keeping, monitoring, and reporting.
To retain coverage under the general permit, permit holders must continually meet all the qualifying criteria and requirements. If at any time the permit holder fails to meet the qualifying criteria and requirements, Ohio EPA can revoke the general permit and require the permit holder to apply for a traditional permit.

Frequently Asked Questions
How does the general permit differ from traditional permits?
The general permit program was developed to improve efficiency. Under the general permit program, all emissions limitations, monitoring, record keeping, reporting, and other requirements for qualifying air pollution sources are developed in advance.
If the applicant's air pollution source meets the qualifying criteria for a specific model general permit and they can comply with all the predefined terms and conditions, Ohio EPA will issue a general permit for the identified air pollution source.
For example, two model general permits are available for concrete batch plant operations. A facility would select the appropriate model general permit for its planned installation and operation and apply for coverage. If Ohio EPA finds that the
facility’s planned installation meets the model general permit criteria, the facility will be issued a general permit.
What are the benefits?
Reduced permit review and issuance time frames. A traditional PTI can take up to 180 days to process and issue while a general PTIO can be issued in 45 days. The program also helps improve permitting consistency — once a permit is obtained, similar sources or facilities which may be needed in the future should also qualify for coverage under the same model general permit.
How are standard emissions limits determined?
Under the traditional PTI and PTIO process, Ohio EPA reviews air pollutant source information, such as potential to emit, and calculates a specific emission limitation for each individual source.
Potential applicants must be sure that their proposed air pollutant source can be installed and/or operated to meet the designated criteria. Model general permit requirements are very specific and designed to ensure that only similar, well-defined sources can be covered under the appropriate category.
For example, one specific model general permit for a natural gas and No. 2 oil-fired boiler is based on a source with a maximum design heat input greater than or equal to 20 mm BTU/hr, but less than or equal to 29.9 mm BTU/hr. The acceptable emissions for this model general permit are based on the potential to emit for sources which fall into the well- defined size limitation.
Is there a fee?
Yes. The fees for coverage under the general permit program are the same as for the traditional PTI and PTIO program. Permit issuance fees are based on the size and type of air pollution source. Emissions fees are based on the emissions generated by the covered source.
Can existing permit holders switch to a general permit?
Yes. If a company already holds a PTI for a qualifying air pollution source, they may apply for issuance of a general permit- to-operate rather than a traditional PTIO. Companies that already hold a PTIO for a qualifying source may request coverage under the general permit program at the time of renewal. In these cases, a previously issued PTI may need to be modified to ensure consistency with the general PTI.
How often do general permits need to be renewed?
A general PTI is effective for the life of the air pollution source. A general PTIO must be renewed every five or 10 years.
Can a source that qualifies for coverage choose to get a traditional PTI and PTIO?
Yes. An applicant may elect to obtain a traditional air PTI and PTIO.
Does the program have a negative effect on air quality?
No. The general permit program is just one tool used to help improve air quality. Each model general permit is specifically designed to cover a distinct, qualifying air pollutant source, and contains the same record keeping, monitoring, and reporting requirements as traditional permits.
How does Ohio EPA ensure permit conditions remain current?
At a minimum, model general permits are reviewed every five years from the date they were originally developed. If needed, Ohio EPA or the public can request that new requirements be incorporated into a model general permit before the five-year review. In addition to the review of model general permits, all issued general permits-to-operate are subject to the same review and renewal every five or 10 years as the traditional PTIO program. Permits may be updated at renewal to conform with new or updated requirements.
How does the general permit affect Ohio EPA's enforcement authority?
Ohio EPA retains the authority to enforce all provisions of the general permit program through the Ohio Administrative Code (OAC). Sources operating under a general permit can be inspected by Ohio EPA and are subject to the same enforcement provisions as other permitted sources.
How to Apply
Applicants should review the qualifying criteria and the terms and conditions of the applicable model general permit to ensure they qualify and are able to comply with the specified terms and conditions. If the model general permit requirements are not acceptable, applicants should apply for a traditional permit.
If the predefined terms and conditions are acceptable, the permit applicant applies for either a general PTI or a general PTO. The applicant only needs to complete the portions of the traditional application that are identified in the application instructions for the specific model general permit of interest.
The terms and conditions of a previously issued permit, for example a PTI, can be modified if necessary for the source to comply with the terms and conditions of the model GP. If this is the case, contact your district office or local air agency for additional information.
Notification of well completion reports, required by the New Source Performance Standards Subpart OOOO, should be submitted to OEPA_subpartOOOO_reports@epa.ohio.gov at least two days before the commencement of well completion operations. This report must contain the information identified in 40 CFR 60.5420(a)(2); and it is recommended that you include the county and the facility ID number, if available.
When the applicant is ready to apply, the following steps should be followed:
Complete and sign the qualifying criteria document. The same signatory for the application should sign this document.
Complete the application and any emissions activity category (EAC) form(s). Be sure to read the application instructions for the specific model general permit for which you are applying. It will provide the applicant with information regarding which sections of the application and EAC form(s) must be completed. Have the signatory, as defined in Ohio Administrative Code (OAC) rule 3745-31-04(B), sign the application.
Include a cover letter that states specifically which model general permit you are applying for, using the "Source Description" and "MGP Number" contained on the qualifying criteria document.
Submit a hardcopy of all documents to the district office or local air agency . IMPORTANT: You have the option of applying through Ohio EPA's online Air Services . Applying online eliminates the need to sign and mail paper copies of these forms.
Ohio expects to be able to issue the model GP within 45 days of receipt of a complete application.
