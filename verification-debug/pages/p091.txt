Air Permitting Fact Sheet
Any person who plans to construct a new facility or modify an existing facility which
emits air contaminants into the atmosphere shall obtain authorization to do so from
the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ). The type of authorization
required for a facility is determined by the type of facility and the amount of
pollutant(s) emitted. The list of potential air quality authorizations, in order of
increasing emissions, are as follows:
•

De Minimis Facilities or Sources – Facilities or sources that meet the
conditions of Title 30, Texas Administrative Code (30 TAC) Section
116.119(a) are considered de minimis and do not have to obtain any
registration or authorization prior to construction.

•

Permit by Rule Authorizations – An operation may qualify for a permit by
rule (PBR) authorization if it emits less than:
o

250 tons per year (tpy) of carbon monoxide or nitrogen oxides.

o

25 tpy of volatile organic compounds, sulfur dioxide, or inhalable
particulate matter.

o

15 tpy of particulate matter with diameters of 10 microns or less.

o

10 tpy of particulate matter with diameters of 2.5 microns or less.

o

25 tpy of any other air contaminant [except carbon dioxide, water,
nitrogen, ethane, hydrogen, oxygen, and greenhouse gases (GHGs),
unless there is a specific provision in an individual PBR to the
contrary].

The general requirements and specific PBRs are found in 30 TAC
Chapter 106. There are 108 individual PBRs that may be claimed or
registered. Note that the facility must meet all the established PBR
requirements to claim a PBR.
•

Standard Permits – Standard permits are authorized under 30 TAC Chapter
116, Subchapter F and are available for the following project types:
anhydrous ammonia storage and distribution operations; animal carcass
incinerators; boilers; concrete batch plants; concrete batch plants with
enhanced controls; cotton gins and cotton burr tub grinders; dry bulk
fertilizer handling; electric generating units; feed mills, portable augers, and
hay grinders; grain elevators, grain handling, and portable grain augers;
marine loading operations; peanut-handling; municipal solid waste landfills;
oil and gas facilities; pollution control projects; sawmills; temporary and
permanent hot mix asphalt plants; temporary and permanent polyphosphate
blenders; and temporary and permanent rock and concrete crushers. Owners
or operators of facilities that meet the established standard permit criteria

•

New Source Review (NSR) Permits – Owners or operators of facilities
that do not qualify for PBRs or standard permits can submit an NSR case-bycase permit application (30 TAC Chapter 116). After receiving a complete
application, TCEQ staff will begin an administrative and technical review.
During the administrative review, staff will determine if public notice is
required and if so, will send the applicant the public notice package for the
publication and posting of signs. The publication starts a 30-day comment
period. The technical review primarily relates to source identification and air
emission quantification, analysis of the off-property health impacts of those
emissions, determination of best available control technology, and the
applicability of any source category or emission-based state and federal
regulations. The applicant may also be required to publish a second public
notice and post signs around the proposed facility location for an additional
30-day comment period.

•

Flexible Permits – A flexible permit allows an owner or operator more
flexibility in managing operations by staying under an overall emissions cap
or individual emission limitation. The owner or operator is allowed to
structure the flexible permit to best serve their needs. Flexible permits follow
the same permitting requirements for NSR permits.

•

Prevention of Significant Deterioration (PSD) Permits – If a new facility
is a major stationary source (or if construction is a major modification) for an
NSR regulated pollutant other than criteria pollutants or located in an
attainment or unclassifiable area for the criteria pollutant, a PSD permit is
required. In addition to the requirements for NSR permits, the PSD review
will require additional modeling. If emissions of GHGs from new construction
or major modification do not meet or exceed the thresholds in 30 TAC
116.164(a)(1) or (a)(2), no authorization for those GHG emissions is
required, otherwise a GHG PSD permit is also required.

•

Nonattainment Permits – If the facility is located in a nonattainment area
designated by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, additional
permitting requirements may apply. In addition to the requirements
discussed for NSR permits, a nonattainment permit review is required if the
facility has emissions above the major source (for new major sources) or
significant thresholds (for existing major sources) for the specific
nonattainment area and criteria pollutant or precursor. Nonattainment
permitting requires the installation of the lowest achievable emission rate
control technology and the acquisition of emission reductions to offset
proposed emissions increases of the criteria pollutant, as well as its
precursors, before the start of operations.

Title V Federal Operating Permits – The owner or operator of major facilities,
per 30 TAC Chapter 122, must submit an abbreviated Title V permit application
before the start-up of a facility. TCEQ shall inform the applicant in writing of the
deadline for submitting the remaining information. Submittal of a Title V permit
application will not affect the start-up of the facility.
