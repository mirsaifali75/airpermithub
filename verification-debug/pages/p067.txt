Title V Permitting Program
October 09, 2021 
| 
Agency 
Title V of the federal Clean Air Act reauthorization (1990) requires each state to develop a permit-to-operate system and emission fee program for major sources of air pollution. Major sources must certify compliance with the terms of their permits annually.
The Title V permit for major sources is enforceable by Ohio EPA and U.S. EPA.
What is a major air pollution source?
Under Title V, major sources are those with a potential to emit (PDF) :
100 tons per year or more of any one regulated pollutant [particulate matter less than 10 microns in diameter (PM10); nitrogen oxides (NOx); sulfur dioxide (SO2); carbon monoxide (CO); volatile organic compounds (VOCs); and lead (Pb)]
10 tons per year or more of any one hazardous air pollutant (HAP)
25 tons per year or more of any two or more HAPs — U.S. EPA currently lists 188 HAPs in Section 112 of the 1990 Clean Air Act
What are the monitoring, reporting, compliance, and certification requirements?
A Title V permit includes emission limits and standards, as well as monitoring, record keeping, reporting, and testing requirements. Records of required monitoring must be maintained for five years. All Title V permit holders must certify annually whether they have complied with the terms of their Title V permit.
Title V facility responsible officials must submit quarterly and semi-annual deviations of all applicable requirements, unless otherwise required by regulation or law.
What are the fees?
The fee for the reporting year 2025 is $65.38/ton in addition to the $5,000 base fee.
Fees are assessed on the actual amount of emissions of particulate matter (PM), sulfur dioxide, nitrogen oxides, organic compounds, and lead. Fees are calculated based on a base fee of $25/ton in 1989 dollars. This fee is subject to annual increases as measured against the 1989 Consumer Price Index.

Title V Fee Report Submission 
Engineering Guide 71 (PDF) on emissions reporting requirements for the fee emission report Ohio Administrative Code (OAC) Chapter 3745-78) for small (<1.0 TPY) and "de minimis" emission units
The emissions fee rules are contained in OAC chapter 3745-78 and Division 3745.11 of the Ohio Revised Code. Learn more about Title V fees on the U.S. EPA website .
By April 15 each year, the facility must submit a fee emission report through Air Services . The report must contain the facility's actual emissions of each regulated pollutant for the previous calendar year.
I'm a small business — is help available?
Ohio EPA offers technical assistance, an ombudsman to represent business concerns, and a clearinghouse of information regarding compliance methods and technologies. For more information, please call 614.387.2234 or email Bradley.Hauser@epa.ohio.gov .
How do I apply?
Title V and Synthetic Minor Title V facilities must use the eBusiness Center's Air Services . Permit renewals must be filed between six and 18 months prior to expiration of the current permit. For details about information used when reviewing Title V renewal applications visit the Resource Hub .

Historical
Final Approval of Ohio EPA's Title V program — Aug. 15, 1995, Federal Register 
Title V permits identify all "applicable requirements" that are established through facility compliance with requirements based on the approved Ohio State Implementation Plan 
Ohio's Title V rules can be found in the Ohio Administrative Code (OAC) 3745-77 
Original Title V Application Deadlines 
To phase in the new Title V permitting program, application deadlines were staggered depending on a facility's location. Ohio Administrative Code (OAC) Chapter 3745-77-04 contains the deadlines for each county or ZIP code location. The deadlines were:
Appendix A (with 90-day extension) - Feb. 27, 1996
Appendix B - March 28, 1996
Appendix B (with 90-day extension) - June 26, 1996
Appendix C - Sept. 30, 1996. This deadline was extended from the original deadline of Sept. 29, 1996
Facilities that meet the major source threshold after the initial appellation deadline are required to file an initial Title V application within the 12 months after the source becomes subject to the Title V permit program.
