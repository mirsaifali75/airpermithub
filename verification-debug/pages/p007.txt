Overview
The 1990 federal Clean Air Act Amendments created the Title V section. It contains the operating permit program, which applies to specific stationary sources of air pollution. 
Facilities that need Title V operating permits may be subject to several air quality requirements. These may include and are not limited to:
Construction permit requirements.
State regulations.
Federal regulations.
Consent decrees.
Title V operating permits make air quality requirements easier to enforce by consolidating all applicable requirements for a source into a single document. The CDPHE Air Pollution Control Division’s Title V operating permits detail exactly how each legal requirement applies to each individual emission point or piece of equipment at a source. Title V operating permits must include sufficient monitoring, recordkeeping, and reporting requirements to establish the source’s compliance with all requirements.
Title V operating permit process
The division follows several steps to ensure it issues legally strong Title V operating permits. The steps for initial or renewed Title V operating permits include:
Draft a new or renewed Title V operating permit.
Make the draft Title V operating permit available for public feedback for at least 30 days and, if requested, hold public hearings with the Colorado Air Quality Control Commission .
Respond to comments received on the draft Title V operating permit.
Submit the draft Title V operating permit, all comments, and all division responses to the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency for a 45-day review period.
Issue the Title V operating permit after federal review is complete.
Title V facilities must apply to renew their operating permits every five years. 
As long as a source submits a complete renewal application on time, its existing Title V operating permit remains in full effect until the division completes the renewal.
The division can continue to enforce a source’s existing Title V operating permit as needed while working on the renewal. 
Each initial and renewed operating permit, as well as significant modifications, are subject to public comment requirements and an opportunity for a hearing.
The division is required by law to recover the costs of the Title V operating permit program by charging applicants a processing fee. For more information, visit the emissions and permitting fees web page .
Applicability
Sources that meet the definition of Major Source in Colorado Air Quality Regulation 3 , Part A, Section I.B.30 are subject to the Title V operating permit program. 
The following stationary sources are major sources of air pollution:
Sources that directly emit, or have the potential to emit: 10 tons per year or more of any single hazardous air pollutant; or
25 tons per year or more of any combination of hazardous air pollutants.
Sources that directly emit, or have the potential to emit: 100 tons per year or more of any pollutant subject to regulation.
Sources subject to the federal Acid Rain Program .
Sources subject to specific New Source Performance Standards under Colorado Air Quality Regulation 6 . For example, landfills that are subject to Subpart Cf and XXX.
Sources that meet the definition of Major Stationary Source in Colorado Air Quality Regulation 3 , Part D, Section II.A.25.
In the Denver Metro/North Front Range area of Colorado, the major stationary source thresholds are lower. A source in that area that emits or has the potential to emit the following is a major stationary source and subject to the Title V operating permit program:
25 tons per year or more of nitrogen oxides (NOx); or 
25 tons per year or more of volatile organic compounds (VOCs) . 
NOx and VOCs are air pollutants that may create ground-level ozone pollution when mixed with sunlight and heat. The Denver Metro/North Front Range area does not currently meet federal health-based standards for ozone pollution .
Title V operating permits company index
Each permit has two associated files.
The first file is a copy of the current operating permit.
If a permit is modified, only the most recent version will appear here.
The second file is a copy of the Technical Review document.
This document is created by the permit review engineer to provide: An overview of the facility.
A general discussion of any important technical, regulatory, or compliance issues encountered during permit processing.
If a permit is modified and an additional Technical Review document is generated, it will be added to the facility's files. Any previous Technical Review documents will also be maintained.
If you have any trouble viewing or downloading files, try updating your web browser or using Google Chrome.
