May 2026 update: Electronic air quality forms 
The CDPHE Air Pollution Control Division is continuing its transition to electronic forms in 2026. Visit the electronic submission page to determine if the form you need is available electronically. If the electronic form is not listed, complete and submit the PDF version via email or mail.
Starting June 30, 2026, all applicants must use the online submission tool for available forms, including Air Pollutant Emission Notices (APENs) and construction permit applications. This means applicants must submit all new APEN and construction permit applications using the online tool. All Title V (major source) operators applying for a construction permit must also use the online tool.
On June 1, the division will remove applicable PDF versions of air forms from its web pages. Applicants must use the online submission tool starting June 30 for applicable air form submissions, including for APENs and construction permits.
The division’s online submission tool now includes electronic forms for: 
All APENs. Note: The Facility-wide Greenhouse Gas Emissions APEN (Form APCD-235) will remain open for PDF submission until further notice.
All construction permit applications.
Other required forms, including: Environmental Justice Summary.
Modeling Requirements Determination (Form APCD-114).
Notice of Startup.
Relocation Notice.
The division anticipates launching an electronic submission process for Title V operating permit applications by the end of 2026. The process will use the same online submission tool currently available for APEN and construction permit applications.
Background 
An APEN is an Air Pollutant Emission Notice, and is used to:
Report air pollution emissions.
Apply for a permit.
Modify an existing permit.
First, determine if you need an APEN. Then, determine if you need a permit.
Any business in Colorado that emits air pollution may be required to report its emissions and/or apply for a permit. Submitting an APEN is required to report your emissions, apply for a permit, or modify a permit based on the amount of annual air emissions. An air permit is required before you begin constructing a new source or modifying an existing stationary source of air pollution.

Reporting air emissions and applying for air permits: Step-by-step guidance
The Reporting Your Air Emissions and Applying for Air Permits guide is organized in two parts, outlined in the full guidance document.
Part I – Getting Started is designed to help you determine whether you need to submit an APEN to the Air Pollution Control Division. 
Part II – Obtaining a Permit is designed to help you determine whether you need an air permit and how to begin the permitting process.
Read the full detailed guidance document for more information.
Guidance at-a-glance 
Below is a summary of the steps outlined in the detailed guidance document.
Expand All
Step 1: Evaluate your operations.
Inventory your operations to identify sources of air pollution emissions at your facility.
Key question to consider: What are the sources of air pollution at this facility?
Step 2: Identify your regulated or reportable pollutants.
Determine whether your operations emit pollutants regulated by the federal government or by the State of Colorado. “Criteria pollutants” and certain “non-criteria pollutants” are regulated. It’s important that you understand which pollutants are emitted from your business operations and how they are categorized.
Key questions to consider: 
What pollutants are being emitted?
What federal and/or state requirements apply?
Criteria pollutants include: 
Carbon monoxide.
Fluorides.
Hydrogen sulfide.
Lead.
Municipal waste combustor emissions.
Nitrogen oxides (NOx).
Ozone.
Particulate matter (PM10, PM2.5, total suspended particulates).
Reduced sulfur compounds.
Sulfur dioxide.
Sulfuric acid mist.
Total reduced sulfur.
Volatile organic compounds (VOCs).
A list of reportable non-criteria pollutants is in Air Quality Control Commission Regulation 3, Appendix B and a list of reportable air toxics are listed in Appendix C.
Annual facility-wide greenhouse gas reporting may also apply. 
Learn more about Greenhouse gas Air Pollutant Emissions Notice reporting and fee rulemakings. 
Step 3: Determine if specific control technology, emissions standards, or performance standards are required for your business.
If your business emits a federally regulated non-criteria pollutant (called a HAP, or hazardous air pollutant, in Regulation 3, Appendix B), then:
The source emitting that pollutant may be subject to EPA’s Maximum Achievable Control Technology (MACT) requirements or a National Emissions Standard for Hazardous Air Pollutants (NESHAP).
MACT/NESHAPs are federal rules that specifically limit the emissions of hazardous air pollutants.
If your business emits a state-regulated priority air toxic in Regulation 30, the source may also be subject to Colorado Priority Air Toxic Emission Control Regulations .
Review all state regulations to determine whether they apply to your facility or operations.
New Source Performance Standards (NSPS) specifically limit the emissions of criteria pollutants.
For more information, read the detailed guidance document .
Step 4: Check if your source is exempt.
A full list of the sources that don’t need to submit an APEN is in Regulation 3 , Part A.II.D. (APEN-exempt) and the APEN and permitting exemptions web page .
If your source type is on these lists, you don’t need to submit an APEN or potentially get a permit, depending on the type of exemption.
If your source is not on the list, your emissions may still be below the threshold that requires an APEN. Calculate your emissions to determine if you need to submit an APEN.
Step 5: Calculate your emissions.
Guidance for calculating your emissions .
We provide resources such as guidance, tools, and policy memos on our APEN, reporting, and air permitting guidance for small businesses web page . Information is categorized by equipment type or source type to help you calculate your emissions.
Step 6: Determine if you need to submit an APEN based on your emissions.
You must first determine if your source is located in the ozone nonattainment area. The Denver Metro/North Front Range area is considered to be nonattainment for the National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS) for ozone. 
Sources in the 8-hour ozone nonattainment must submit an APEN if VOCs and/or NOx emissions are above their nonattainment area thresholds. The nonattainment threshold only applies to VOCs and NOx. Emissions for all other pollutants should be compared to the attainment threshold. Sources outside the 8-hour ozone nonattainment area should compare emissions to the attainment thresholds. 
For more information about the 8-hour ozone nonattainment area, refer to the air division’s fact sheet or interactive map . If you are still unsure if your facility is in the nonattainment area, contact the division .
If your emission rate is higher than APEN thresholds in the table (and your source does not qualify for an exemption based on something other than emissions), you must submit an APEN. The uncontrolled actual emissions in a calendar year must be compared to the APEN threshold. Uncontrolled actual emissions don’t account for any existing pollution control equipment.
If your emission rate is below APEN thresholds, you don't have to submit anything to the air division. Continue tracking your emissions and revisit this page if your emissions go above APEN thresholds.
When considering permit thresholds, all sources of emissions at the same site that exceed APEN thresholds for a given pollutant must be added together and compared to the appropriate permit threshold for that pollutant.

Steps 7 and 8: Determine if you need an air permit.
If you do not need an air permit, complete and submit APEN forms and fees .
If you need an air permit, go to Step 9. 
If your source requires an APEN, you might need a permit. Just like with APENs, a source can be exempt from permitting based on:
Type of emissions source.
Amount of annual emissions.
If you have multiple sources at your facility, consult the detailed guidance .
A full list of the sources that don’t need a permit is in Regulation 3, Part B.II.D. If your source type is on this list, you don’t need to get a permit. If your source requires an APEN, you must still submit one. For a list of frequent exemptions, visit the APEN and air permit exemptions web page .
If your source type is not listed as permit-exempt in Regulation 3, Part B.II.D., your emissions still may be below the threshold for requiring a permit. The permit emissions thresholds are in the table above. Note that the permit thresholds apply to your entire facility, not just the source you are reporting.
Add the emissions you calculated to the emissions from all other emission sources at that same facility that require an APEN. If that total meets or exceeds the permit thresholds in Table 1, a permit is required along with applicable Environmental Justice Summary and Modeling Requirements Determination forms .
Step 9: Submit an APEN or apply for a permit.
Access APENS and permits available via the electronic submission tool. 
Explore APENs and resources to apply for, modify, or renew a permit on the non-oil and gas air permits web page .
Explore oil and gas air permits information, such as forms to apply or modify, including APENs, and permit process guidance.
Step 10: Next steps for permit-required sources
After the division issues a construction permit, the owner or operator may be required to:
File a Notice of Startup for the associated equipment.
Complete the self-certification process.
Submit a Relocation Notice for portable sources.
Renew APEN (s) at least every 5 years, or report emissions annually if applicable.
Refer to the post permit submission requirements and continuous compliance web page for more detailed information.
Notice of Startup
Submit the Notice of Startup using the division’s online submission tool.
You must submit a Notice of Startup within 15 days of:
Commencement of operation, as defined in Regulation 3 I.B.15 or
The permit issuance date.
The Notice of Startup form must include the specific equipment details that were not provided in the permit application. This includes the make, model, and serial number of the emissions source and emissions-control equipment.
Self-certification
The final step of the air permitting process requires the owner or operator to submit self-certification. Within 180 days of receiving the Initial Approval permit or commencing operation, the owner or operator must certify compliance with the permit requirements to receive a Final Approval to Operate letter.
The owner or operator must submit the self-certification documentation within 180 days of commencing operations, or the permit may be revoked. The permit will specify the requirements for self-certification.
Self-certification information must be emailed to cdphe_apcd_selfcertification@state.co.us .
Portable Source Relocation Notice
To report the relocation of permitted portable air pollution sources, you must submit the Relocation Notice at least 10 days before the relocation, unless a different arrangement has been made with the division. Submit the form with the online submission tool.
APEN renewals and updates
You must update your APEN every five years, or sooner if:
Your actual emissions increased significantly from the levels reported on a previous APEN, according to Regulation 3, Part A, Section II.C.2.
You have changes in process or equipment, including any change in ownership.
For oil and gas sources: Starting Jan. 1, 2027, annual emission report (Emissions Reporting Notice) submission under Regulation 7, Part B, Section V. will meet the requirement to submit an APEN renewal at the expiration of each five-year term, or because of a significant change in actual emissions.
While Emission Reporting Notices may satisfy most APEN renewals, if you have changes in process or equipment, including any change in ownership, you must still submit an APEN.

Annual emissions reports and Emission Reporting Notices
Annual emissions reports are due on June 30 each year. The reports include data for the previous calendar year. 
Oil and gas: 
All sources located at or upstream of gas processing plants must report emissions annually by submitting an Emissions Reporting Notice (Regulation 7, Part B, Section V). 
Visit the Oil and Natural Gas Annual Emission Inventory Reporting (ONGAEIR) web page for more information. 
Non-oil and gas: 
Outside of the oil and gas sector, all Title V and synthetic minor sources must report annual facility-wide air toxics emissions. Starting with calendar year 2026 emissions, annual reporting will expand to include additional APEN-reportable pollutants and certain other minor sources to align with requirements finalized in Regulation 3, Part A, Section IX. 
Visit the annual air toxics reports guidance web page for more information. 
Complete application and rejection criteria 
Starting June 30, 2026, all applicants must use the online submission tool for available forms, including APENs and construction permit applications. The air division rejects incomplete APENs and permit applications.
The division’s online submission tool now includes electronic forms for: 
All APENs. The Facility-wide Greenhouse Gas Emissions APEN (Form APCD-235) will remain open for PDF submission until further notice.
All construction permit applications.
Other required forms, including: Environmental Justice Summary.
Modeling Requirements Determination (Form APCD-114).
Notice of Startup.
Relocation Notice.
Use the applicability tool to determine if you need to submit the Environmental Justice Summary or Modeling Requirements Determination Form APCD-114. 
Using your phone? Use the applicability tool made specifically for mobile phones .
To ensure your application is complete, use the most recent Permit Application/Registration Checklist (Form APCD-100) if required.
This form may be required for some APENs and air permits submitted using the online submission tool. 
Form APCD-100 must be attached to the APEN submission if required. APENs for Well Production Facility (GP09/GP10) require Form APCD-100, for instance.
If you have questions about the applicability of Form APCD-100, contact the division .
Your application will be rejected if it: 
Is filled out incorrectly.
Is missing information.
Is missing the required forms.
Lacks payment for the filing fee.
If an application is rejected, the division will require the applicant to formally resubmit the entire application with all required documents. The applicant must repay any relevant fee(s) with each new application submission.
The division works to identify incomplete applications as quickly as possible after receipt. The division then issues a formal written rejection of the application.

Important reminders for Title V operating permits
The division considers Title V operating permit applications without the required Environmental Justice Summary to be incomplete. If the application is incomplete, the source will not be granted an application shield. Title V forms, instructions, and guidance documents were updated effective August 2023.
Visit the Title V operating permits web page for more information. 
Application exemptions
Permit Application/Registration Checklist (Form APCD-100)
The Permit Application/Registration Checklist (Form APCD-100) may be required for some APENs submitted using the online submission tool. For more information, visit the electronic submission frequently asked questions page .
The following application types are exempt from the requirement to submit Form APCD-100 :
APEN updates only (no changes to permit). Renewal of 5-year APEN term.
Update to actual annual emissions, per Regulation 3, Part A, Section II.C.
APEN exempt sources.
Permit exempt sources.
Administrative permit amendments. Including but not necessarily limited to name changes, changes in contact information, transfers of ownership, and typographical error corrections.
If you have questions about the applicability of Form APCD-100, contact the division .
