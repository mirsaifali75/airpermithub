Air quality permitting 
The Bureau of Air Pollution Control (BAPC) issues air quality permits for stationary and temporary sources. These permits make sure emissions do not harm public health or reduce air quality. 
When you need an air quality permit
Any process or activity that produces emissions may need a permit.
Nevada law defines:
An emission source as any property that emits or may emit an air contaminant Visit NRS 445B.155 (external link)
An air contaminant as any substance released into the air except water vapor and droplets Visit NRS 445B.110 (external link)
You do not need a permit if your equipment, activities, or storage containers release only steam or water particles.
Permit types and thresholds
Use the following thresholds as a guide. Actual requirements may vary based on facility conditions.
Class I permits
Typically required for facilities that:
Emit more than 100 tons per year of any regulated pollutant, or
Emit more than 25 tons per year of total Hazardous Air Pollutants (HAP), or
Emit more than 10 tons per year of any single HAP, or
Are Prevention of Significant Deterioration (PSD) sources or major Maximum Achievable Control Technology (MACT) sources
Class II permits
Typically required for facilities that:
Emit less than 100 tons per year of any regulated pollutant, and
Emit less than 25 tons per year of total HAP, and
Emit less than 10 tons per year of any single HAP
Surface Area Disturbance (SAD) permits
Required when land disturbance is greater than 5 acres 
General and Change of Location Approval (COLA) permits
For portable construction equipment used for less than 12 months at one location
Often used for road and highway projects
Mercury permits
Required for owners or operators that operate, construct, or modify a thermal unit that emits mercury
Construction of a new or modified thermal unit that emits mercury requires a permit before construction beings 
Any facility subject to 40 CFR Part 63 Subpart EEEEEEE must obtain a Title V (Class I) permit, regardless of the size of the facility
Visit Nevada Mercury Control Program (NMCP) - Support Documents
Chemical Accident Prevention Program (CAPP) permits
Applies in facilities that have select, highly hazardous substances in quantities above defined thresholds
Applies to facilities that manufacture explosives for sale that are classified as division 1.1 through 1.5 in the Table of Hazardous Material under 49 CFR 172.101 Visit 49 CFR 172.101 (external link)
Visit Chemical Accident Prevention Program (CAPP) 
Need help?
Contact the permitting branch at (775) 687‑9349
